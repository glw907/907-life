Just finished Richard Powers' *The Overstory*. It's a sprawling novel that weaves together multiple storylines, all connected by trees and the people who love them.

## What Struck Me

The book operates on multiple timescales simultaneously. There are human lives measured in decades, and tree lives measured in centuries. Powers shifts between them effortlessly, making you feel the weight of deep time.

The central question: What happens when you really pay attention to the living world around you?

## Favorite Passages

> "The best arguments in the world won't change a person's mind. The only thing that can do that is a good story."

This line kept coming back to me. Powers doesn't lecture about environmental destruction; he tells stories that make you see trees as characters with their own agency and narrative arcs.

## Recommended?

Yes, especially if you're patient with literary fiction. It's long and slow-moving, and it rewards attention.
