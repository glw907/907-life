I've been thinking about attention lately. Not productivity hacks or focus techniques, but the deeper question of what it means to direct your awareness toward something.

## The Observation

I notice that when I walk the same trail every day, I stop seeing it. My mind wanders. I'm physically present but mentally absent, running through todo lists or replaying conversations.

But then something breaks the pattern—unusual light, a bird call, fresh snow—and suddenly I'm *there* again. Fully present. The trail becomes new.

## The Question

What if attention isn't something we have or lack, but something we practice? Like a muscle that atrophies without use?

The modern environment seems designed to scatter attention rather than cultivate it. Infinite feeds, constant notifications, the expectation of immediate response. Everything pulling us toward surface-level engagement.

## The Experiment

For the past month, I've been leaving my phone at home during morning walks. Simple change, but the effect has been noticeable. Without the option to check messages or take photos, I just... walk. And notice things.

This morning: ice crystals on a dead fireweed stalk, catching the low sunlight. I would have missed it entirely with a screen in my pocket.

No grand conclusions yet. Just noticing what happens when I remove one small source of distraction.
