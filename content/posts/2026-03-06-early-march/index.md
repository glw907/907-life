There's a week or two every year when Anchorage can't decide what it's doing. The sun has climbed back up to somewhere meaningful — you notice it now in late afternoon, slanting gold across the snowpack — but the temperatures haven't gotten the message yet. It dropped to nine below on Tuesday. Wednesday I was walking without a hat.

I've come to think of early March as its own season here, separate from either winter or spring. The snow is still deep but it's changed character: it's denser now, with a crust that holds your weight until it doesn't. The birches are still bare but something in the color of the bark has shifted, gone more bronze than gray. You can't point to any single thing and say *there*, that's it, that's the change — it's more that the light comes from a different angle and makes everything feel slightly rearranged.

I took the dog out along the greenbelt this morning before the temperature had risen above fifteen. We had the trail mostly to ourselves. She found something under a spruce tree that required extensive investigation while I stood and watched my breath and thought about nothing in particular, which is harder than it sounds and also one of the few things I'm reliably good at in the morning.

There's a red squirrel that lives in the woodpile off the back deck and reappears every March like a small, aggressive sign of life. He was back yesterday, scolding from the top of the stack. I took this as a good omen. By the time he quieted down the sun had moved behind the ridge and the temperature dropped two degrees in about ten minutes, which is the other thing March does — remind you not to get comfortable.

Spring here doesn't arrive so much as it negotiates. But March is when you know, in some provisional way, that it's coming.
